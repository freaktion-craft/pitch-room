Measurements
For a fair comparison
  • Compare the same note (within a semitone) and the same vowel. Almost every measure shifts with pitch and vowel.
  • Always record yourself with the same mic at the same distance. Volume doesn’t matter (only differences are compared), but the mic’s colour does.
  • Vocals pulled from a released track carry mixing (EQ, reverb, compression), so trust only the rough direction. Any accompaniment in the clip throws the analysis off.
  • Above about 350 Hz (around F4) vowel formants can’t be measured reliably, so the vowel chart is greyed out.

Good to know

      
    
        
  • Warm up for a few minutes first. A cold voice loses a few notes at both ends.
    • 
        
  • The bars are typical ranges for trained singers. Untrained voices usually stop a few notes short at the top, so the lowest note decides here and the top only nudges it.
    • 
        
  • Range is only part of it. Teachers also listen for where your voice changes gear (the passaggio) and for its colour, so treat this as a starting point.
    • 
        
  • M1 and M2 are the two main ways the vocal folds vibrate: M1 is chest and mixed voice, M2 is head voice and falsetto. The typical tops for bass, baritone and tenor are M1 notes; for alto, mezzo and soprano they are M2 notes, so for bass to tenor only your M1 top counts.
    • 
        
  • The app tells M1 from M2 by the sound: in M2 the first harmonic gets much stronger and the high ones fade, compared with how you sounded at the bottom of the same slide. It’s a guess from sound alone (studies use a sensor on the throat to be sure), and a smooth, trained mix can fool it.
